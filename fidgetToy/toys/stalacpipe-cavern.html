FIELD NOTES / LURAY CAVERNS, 1954–1957

Press a key.
A rock in the dark sings.

In 1954, Pentagon mathematician and electronics scientist Leland W. Sprinkle began searching the 64-acre subterranean chambers of Luray Caverns with a tuning fork and an angle grinder. Over three years, he selected and shaved 37 ancient limestone stalactites spanning 3.5 acres, wiring each formation to a custom console with thin copper relays and hinged rubber-tipped solenoid mallets.

Slide across the 37-key console, or sweep your lantern directly across the cave ceiling to brush the mallets. Every strike sends an electrical pulse along its copper wire, shakes loose mineral droplets into the subterranean mirror pool below, and echoes back according to its depth in the cavern.

Keyboard: Z–M bass octave · A–L middle octave · Q–P upper · Space Cathedral chord · W schematics · Esc hush. On the far right wall hangs a rough 38th stone Leland never wired.
