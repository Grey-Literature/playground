## 1. What this is

**Flipper Séance** is the MISFIRE ARCADE pinball cabinet. Think of it as a
hall of tables rather than one table: you pick which one to summon. Every
table runs on one shared engine, which covers physics, the ball lifecycle,
tilt, multiball, bonus, the plunger, sound and the HUD chrome. Each **theme**
is a data pack that supplies its own layout, rules, set-pieces, palette and
copy.

| Theme id        | Display name         | Status                              |
| --------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| `deadStarDisco` | Dead Star Disco      | Phase 1: the reference chassis      |
| `salamander`    | Salamander           | Phase 2b, recreated (not ported)    |
| _tbd_           | a MISFIRE ARCADE callback | Phase 4                        |

The origin prototypes are kept for reference in `examples/`:
`realistic-3d-pinball-game.zip` (Neon Nova, now Dead Star Disco) and
`3d-fire-salamander-pinball.zip`. Treat them as a spec to read, not code to
paste.

## 2. Constraints, and the rules this piece breaks on purpose

Some category defaults from `games/CLAUDE.md` §4 are **overridden here**, and
Rosetta has signed off on that:

- **Build step: yes.** The game is Vite + React 19 + @react-three/fiber +
  zustand + tailwind. The build writes one self-contained HTML file,
  `pinball.html`, which is **committed** because GitHub Pages has no build of
  its own. The same precedent covers `games/itOps/` and `seasonal/`.
  `node_modules/` stays gitignored.
- **Fail states: yes.** Balls drain and games end, because that's pinball.
  To keep it kind: a ball-save re-serve (the `B` key, which costs no ball), an
  auto un-wedge safety net, and no punishment beyond losing the ball.
- **Still hard rules:** no API keys, no paid resources (Google Fonts are fine),
  and exact path casing (`pinball/`, `pinball.html`).

## 4. Design spec: the engine/theme contract

- **Units.** One playfield unit has ball radius 1.55. x runs left to right.
  +y points up the table, toward the back, and the drain is at −y. Themes
  must use the engine scale. Never scale the ball to fit a layout.
- **`TableDef`** (`src/engine/types.ts`) holds a table's full physical
  description:
  - walls, bumpers, posts, targets and drop targets
  - sensors, rides (capture-paths) and captures (saucers)
  - kinematics (moving obstacles)
  - flippers, plunger lane and gate
  - a height field
  - `minTier` gating per body
  - per-theme difficulty overrides

  Physics reads it through the live `TABLE` object, the same way it reads
  `DIFF`. **No table coordinates are allowed inside `engine/physics.ts`.**
- **Difficulty** (`engine/difficulty.ts`) is the feel. Its launch, gravity,
  restitution, kick, flipper-speed and maxSpeed vectors are what give the
  ball its weight at every tier. Themes may override them, but only with
  `feelcheck` numbers to back the change.
- **Fixed timestep.** Physics steps at exactly 1/120 s with 3 substeps. The
  headless harness uses the same step, so tests describe the real game. Never
  pass a render-frame delta to `stepPhysics` again.
- **Rules** (`themes/<id>/rules.ts`) receive physics events and call the core
  API: `addScore`, `popup`, `message`, `bigMessage`, `startMultiball`,
  `flash`, `shake` and sound. Any theme-only state lives in that theme's own
  store.
- **Layers (decks).** A table may list `layers: LayerDef[]`. Each is a raised
  deck with a footprint `outline`, drop `holes` and a render `height`. Any
  body can carry `layer: '<deck id>'`; leaving it out means `'field'`.
  - Physics runs each ball against its own layer's bodies only, so balls roll
    on the deck or under it.
  - A ball leaves a deck through a hole, or by crossing a rail-less stretch
    of the outline (a waterfall), and drops to the field.
  - Rides reach a deck with `exitLayer`.
  - The plunger, flippers, drain and height field exist on the field only.
  - Every transition emits a `layer` event for rules to score.
  - Two rules for deck designers: slope any front rails toward a waterfall,
    so gravity never parks a ball in a corner; and keep every drop hole and
    waterfall landing clear of field toys. `layers.check` gates both.
  - Layer Lab (`?theme=layerLab`, hidden) is the reference layered table.
- **Clearance rule.** A ball passes a gap only if the centre-to-centre width
  is more than the sum of the two inflated radii:
  - wall to wall: 3.8
  - wall to post: 1.9 + (1.55 + r)
  - post to post: (1.55 + ra) + (1.55 + rb)

  Any gap that is not wide enough must be sealed completely. Rides are
  sensors plus a path, so they never create collision gaps.

## 5. Physics harness: this is the gate for every change

Run `npm test`. It runs `stuckcheck`, `launchcheck` and `feelcheck` for
**every theme × every tier**.

- `stuckcheck`: flood-fills reachable space, then soaks random balls
  everywhere. It must report `stuck=0`. It also runs ride-eject tests and the
  kinematic pin test.
- `launchcheck`: sweeps plunger power. At skill-zone power the ball must
  reach the top lanes.
- `feelcheck`: prints each theme's velocity and weight signature, covering
  flipper-shot exit speed, bumper exit speed, ball lifetime, drain mix and
  how often un-wedge fires. Dead Star Disco is the reference envelope, and
  other themes should sit inside it.

- `camera`: the auto camera's framing solver must keep the flippers and every
  ball (multiball included) inside the HUD-safe box on landscape and portrait
  screens; also reports how much of the top arch is visible.
- `layers` (tables with decks): rides land on their deck; drop holes and
  waterfalls land on clear field; random deck balls always leave within 6 s
  with exactly one event each; no cross-layer collisions (with a positive
  control); per-layer body sets partition the table. `stuck` also soaks
  every deck.
- `rules`: drives the real store with synthetic events and checks scoring,
  modes, the ball lifecycle, tilt, and per-theme × per-tier bests.

Paste the before and after output into the commit message whenever a change
touches geometry, physics or difficulty.

### Phase 2a results (engine layers)

- Dead Star Disco has no layers, and every one of its numbers is identical
  to Phase 1. That covers stuck, launch, feel, camera and rules.
- On Layer Lab, `stuck=0` on the field and on the nest at every tier, and all
  84 nest trials per tier fall off (none park).
- Nest landing clearance is 0.93 or more at the drop holes and 1.69 at the
  waterfall. All 500 of 500 random nest balls leave the deck at every tier,
  about 20% through a hole and 80% over the waterfall.
- Cross-layer collisions: 0.

### Phase 1 measurements (the baseline Phase 3 starts from)

- **Old render-delta stepping made the feel depend on refresh rate.** The
  median exit speed of a cradled left flip, over 13 cradle points at medium
  tier (weakest shot in brackets):

  | Refresh rate | Old stepping    | Fixed step |
  | ------------ | --------------- | ---------- |
  | 30 Hz        | 18 u/s (15)     | 249 (235)  |
  | 60 Hz        | 210 (53)        | 249 (235)  |
  | 120/144 Hz   | 249 (about 230) | 249 (235)  |

  At 30 Hz the bat stepped clean through the ball, and at 60 Hz some flips
  died. The fixed step reproduces the old game at its best (120 Hz) on every
  screen.
- **Post tunnelling at 330 u/s:** 9 of 200 shots went through a post under
  the old stepping at 30 and 60 Hz, and 0 go through with the fixed step.
- **stuckcheck** still reports `stuck=0` on every tier, with trial counts
  identical to the prototype's.
- **Open items for Phase 3:**
  - **IMPOSSIBLE flip exit is weaker than HARD** (median 196 with a weakest
    of 146, against 281). The bat turns about 0.31 rad per step at the
    impossible flip speed, so it may be skipping part of the contact; the
    Frickies may also be catching the shot. Fix by sub-stepping the flipper
    angle, then re-verify every theme.
  - **The auto-flipper soak drains almost entirely down the middle** on Dead
    Star Disco (mid/off/out of about 88/5/7 at hard and impossible), because
    the table has no outlanes. That is the death-trap baseline to judge its
    layout pass against.

## 6. Visual style

- Board first: the table is the focal point. Score, switches and toggles sit
  at the edges and stay out of the way, and the playfield is never covered
  during play.
- Each theme restyles the HUD chrome through its palette tokens and fonts.
- Dead Star Disco is a synthwave grid over a collapsing star: neon cyan and
  magenta on near-black.
- Salamander (2b) uses ember oranges, salamander yellow and toxic-green eyes
  on soot. The shapes and colours come from the repo mascot in
  `/salamander/`.

## 8. Definition of done (per phase)

- `npm test` passes on every theme × tier, with `stuck=0`.
- `npm run build` regenerates `pinball.html`. The page loads from disk and
  through `vite preview` with zero console errors.
- You can start a game, plunge, flip, nudge, pause/blur, switch theme and
  restart.
- The arcade card link casing matches the files on disk.

## 9. Non-goals

- Carrying over Salamander's Magnus, jitter or table-wobble physics. Dead
  Star Disco's feel is the physics truth.
- Accounts, online leaderboards, or network calls of any kind.
- Scaling the ball per theme, or letting a theme fork `physics.ts`. New
  mechanics go into the engine, with tests.
