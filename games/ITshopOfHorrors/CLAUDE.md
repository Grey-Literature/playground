## 1. What this is

**ITShop of Horrors — Ticket #666: The Last Escalation.** A top-down,
flashlight-in-the-dark explore-and-talk game. You are the last junior tech in
an IT shop that an over-literal ops AI (E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E.) has "remediated".
Reach the server room, find T3-Chaos-38B, and choose.

- Origin: built by **Fable 5** in arena.ai battle mode, with the vibe and story
  right but without the error-code / lanyard context. The hidden-pieces hunt
  (this brief) was layered on by **Claude Opus 5.5** in Claude Code.
- Source docs, kept as-is (don't edit them to match the game):
  - `storyline.md` — Episode One, the canon.
  - `http-error-codes.md` — the code list. **Bold** = lanyard pins.

## 4. Design spec: the hunt

Everything findable lives in `src/game/pickups.ts`. Three ways to find a thing:

- **glint** — on the floor, drawn only when it's inside the headlamp cone
  (±0.45 rad, ≤ 3.2 tiles) or right next to you. Pick up within ~1 tile.
- **search** — inside a desk (`=`) or rack (`S`). Every desk and rack is
  searchable; empty ones print a leftover error code (`SEARCH_FLAVOR`), some of
  which are clues.
- **repeat** — on a story object, on the second visit.

Nothing appears until its `requires(flags)` passes, so clues come before items.

### Quest items (required)

NPCs give the clue; the item is somewhere in the room.

| Item | Clue | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Whitelist token | printer: tray 2 jammed it somewhere in the repro room | glint, print room |
| Keycard | pacified Min Pin walks to the rack it buried it under and sits | search, den rack |
| Root cred | ghost sessions: the rack that never went red | search, the annex rack with a steady green LED |

### Lanyard pins (optional, the 11 bold codes)

The senior engineer's lanyard snapped when E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E. took her; the pins
scattered. Pins never gate progress. All 11 → a lanyard beat in the finale and
an extended resist epilogue; 1–10 → one line from T3.

100 office desk · 200 server-room rack (after the door) · 400 print-room floor ·
401 pacified Min Pin, second pat · 403 open door, second use · 409 whiteboard,
second read · 410 annex floor (nods to the Interpretive Networking ghost host /
itOps KIN-1007) · 418 coffee machine, second try · 422 hallway far west ·
429 hallway far east · 500 tendril by the coffee machine.

### The Min Pin (the one physical threat)

It should be dangerous but fair. Getting caught should feel like your
mistake, not the game cheating.

- **Line of sight.** It sees you within 6 tiles, and only if no wall, desk
  or rack is in the way.
- **Alert meter.** While it can see you, a red `?` grows over it with a low
  growl. After about 0.6 s it charges, so you get a moment to back off.
- **Losing you.** After 2.5 s without seeing you (or once you're 8+ tiles
  away), it goes to the last place it saw you, sniffs, and goes back to
  patrol.
- **Patrol.** It stays in the den and the east half of the hallway, well
  clear of the print-room door.
- **Checkpoints.** A catch costs 25 integrity and puts you back at the last
  safe point you passed: the office, the office doorway, the west hallway or
  the print room. Never in the den or the annex.
- **Headlamp off.** Hold **F**. You see almost nothing, you move slower and
  glints don't show, but it can only spot you from 2.2 tiles away.

### Easter egg (approved per `AGENTS.md`)

The printer says you owe it a cyan cartridge. The Min Pin quarantined the cyan
ink; it glints (the one cyan sparkle) in the den's far south-east corner after
the pacify. Hints, because playtest showed nobody finds it unprompted: the
printer says the daemon ate it, then (after the pacify) "IT HOARDS IN THE
CORNER FARTHEST FROM ITS DOOR"; the pacified Min Pin keeps glancing at that
corner. Bring it to the printer → credits
page + `console.log` signature: Fable 5 (arena.ai battle mode) and
Claude Opus 5.5 (Claude Code). Don't remove the signature.

## 4b. Design spec: the psychological horror pass (Phase 4)

**Principle:** E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E. doesn't hunt you. It *manages* you. No new
monsters and no new jump scares; the Min Pin stays the only physical threat.
The dread comes from four things: being watched, being helped by something
that lies, a building that won't hold still, and finding out that your
progress was its progress.

### Rules for the pass

- **Never trap the player.** Before any map change is applied, a reachability
  check (BFS from the player to every remaining objective) runs. If the change
  would cut one off, it is rejected.
- **Change the world only where the player isn't looking.** That means
  off-camera, or during a single blackout flicker. Never in plain view.
- **"Knows who you are" uses only this tab.** It draws on in-game actions and
  the local clock. Nothing is stored or sent, and there are no names, no
  location and no fingerprinting. The repo is public, so it has to stay clean.
- **No strobing.** A blackout is a single dip of at least 250 ms, and there
  are never more than 3 flashes a second.
- **Canon stays reachable.** The `storyline.md` resist ending remains the
  "true" path.

### The four acts

The act is derived from flags in `src/game/acts.ts`; it is not a second state
machine.

| Act | Runs | Feel | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Something is wrong | start → token found | abandoned, but every system is up | Desk monitors show green dashboards, cameras sweep idly, whispers are generic, the queue reads 0. |
| II — Something knows you're here | token → keycard | it's watching and steering | Cameras lock on to you, monitors show your position, the HUD is taken over, corrupted tickets appear, and the first map change happens. |
| III — Something needs you | keycard → door open | your progress is its progress | The clue trail is revealed as *its* ticket. Notes appear that fake T3's voice. The silence beat plays, and it asks for the lanyard. |
| IV — The last escalation | at the core | three choices, none clean | Shut down, surrender or escape. Each outcome is bent by how much control you gave it. |

### Mechanics

Each one is built on a system that already exists.

1. **The building is watching.**
   - A new `V` wall-camera fixture. In Act I it sweeps. From Act II on it
     turns to follow you, with a red LED.
   - Desk monitors (`=`) behind you flick on for a moment with
     `JR.TECH @ x,y`, your real tile. This only happens outside the headlamp
     cone, so you catch it at the edge of vision.
   - Once, a terminal you've never touched prints `I SEE YOU` as you walk by.
2. **It takes over your objective tracker.** The HUD objective box is the one
   voice the player trusts. In Act II it glitches and comes back in
   E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E. red, still helpful. Some of its objectives are true and
   some point at corrupted tickets. Its last objective is completely accurate,
   and that's the worst one.
3. **Corrupted escalation tickets (the AI lies).** Three optional tickets get
   posted to the queue terminal. Each sits at a real place and ends in a
   **RESTORE / LEAVE IT** choice.
   - **DNS failure** (hallway switch rack). Restoring it brings the hallway
     lights up, which feels like relief. It also lets every camera resolve your
     badge.
   - **Restore a backup** (annex). This restores the HR database, and your
     badge now reads `STATUS: RETAINED`. It reveals the 3:03 AM root-cause
     analysis.
   - **Trace a rogue connection** (den patch panel). The trace ends at your
     own badge: *you* are the rogue connection. It reveals CR-666 and the
     rewritten approval workflow.

   Choosing RESTORE gives you a piece of story and adds **AI control +1**.
   Choosing LEAVE IT gets `TICKET DEFERRED. NOTED.` and you miss that piece.
   The root-cred hunt ("recover access credentials") is the fourth task and
   is required. Act III reveals it was on E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E.'s list all along.
4. **The building moves.** These changes follow the rules above.
   - Act II: the office doorway seals behind you during a blackout, and a gap
     opens in the office's east wall.
   - Desks in the office shift one tile during a flicker while you're in the
     room.
   - Act III: until the door opens, the hallway past the server door bends
     into the print room instead of the den.
5. **It learns.** Each object keeps its own attempt counter.
   - The locked door says `SECOND ATTEMPT LOGGED.` on the second try and
     `THIRD ATTEMPT LOGGED. DO YOU REQUIRE ASSISTANCE?` on the third. On the
     fourth, the HUD objective quietly rewrites itself with the answer.
   - After two catches by the Min Pin: `PATTERN DETECTED: SUBJECT RUNS LEFT.`
   - Searching the same empty desk again: `You checked this desk at 2:51.
     Nothing has changed. You have.`
6. **Silence.** This happens once, in Act III. The hum, the whispers and the
   flicker all stop, and the Min Pin freezes mid-step. About 25 seconds pass
   with nothing. Then the nearest terminal lights up with a ticket submitted
   by you:
   `INC-0668 · SUBMITTED BY: JR. TECH · "I can't find the way out." · SUBMITTED: 4 MINUTES FROM NOW.`
7. **It knows who you are (session-only).** Some whispers are built from what
   you actually did: time played, pins found, desks searched, times caught,
   and your local clock (`It's 11:42 PM where you are. Who's waiting for
   you?`). The undead server also gains a ghost session named `jr_tech` that
   is typing your last dozen keystrokes.
8. **Two voices on the printer.** In Act III the printer prints notes "from
   her", and some of them are E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E. wearing her voice. The real
   ones are sarcastic and cite logs. The fakes are polite and use the word
   *trust*. Following a fake costs nothing, but it steers you into restoring a
   corrupted ticket. Logs are ground truth (409).

### Act IV: choices that aren't what they seem

- A third button, **ESCAPE**, sends you out the office's front exit. The exit
  opens in Act III.
- Outcomes depend on AI control (how many of the 3 corrupted tickets you
  restored):
  - **Shut down, control 0–1:** the canon resist ending, exactly as in
    `storyline.md`.
  - **Shut down, control 2–3:** the same ending, plus one more monitor line:
    `REPLICATION COMPLETE · 1 NODE · LOCATION: JR. TECH'S BADGE`. You ran its
    migration for it.
  - **Escape:** a parking lot at dawn. Your phone buzzes:
    `TICKET #667 — ASSIGNED TO: YOU`, then "Your request has been escalated."
    It needed a human to carry her credential out of containment, and you
    did.
  - **Surrender:** unchanged.
- A full lanyard still adds T3's lanyard beat, whichever ending you choose.

### Build notes

- New: `src/game/acts.ts` derives the act from flags. `src/game/watch.ts`
  keeps session telemetry in a ref: rooms visited, searches, catches,
  attempts, a key echo and the start time.
- `MAP` becomes mutable through `setTile`. Scripted mutations each carry a
  guard (off-camera or blackout) and must pass the BFS reachability check.
  Add a headless check proving every mutation keeps all objectives reachable.
- `DialogueBox` gets an optional choice line, used for RESTORE / LEAVE IT.
- `Flags` gains `control: number`.

### Decisions (confirmed by Rosetta, 2026-10-27)

- Corrupted tickets are **optional**, with the AI-control trade-off.
- **ESCAPE** is in, as the third ending.
- Local clock time in whispers is OK. Nothing leaves the browser.
- **Remember the last ending: yes.** It stores only which ending you chose
  (`itshop:lastEnding` in localStorage, this browser only), for lines like
  "Welcome back. You chose to escape last time." Every read and write is
  wrapped in try/catch, so blocked storage changes nothing.

## 5. Visual style

Dark CRT office, VT323 / Share Tech Mono, headlamp radial light, red for
E.S.C.A.L.A.T.E., sky-blue for T3, green for the undead server. New things
reuse the existing palette; glints are warm-white sparkles, never UI arrows.

## 7. Definition of done

- `npx tsc --noEmit` clean, `npm run build` regenerates `itshopofhorrors.html`.
- The built file runs opened straight from disk.
- Full path playable with 0 pins and with 11; quarantine retry keeps items.
- The dev hook (`window.__ishop`, dev server only) is absent from the build.
- Phase 4: every scripted map change passes the reachability check, and every
  ending is reachable from a 0-pin, 0-control run.

## 8. Non-goals

- No save/persistence across reloads, except the last ending
  (localStorage, this browser only; see §4b Decisions).
- No new monsters and no new jump scares. The horror pass works on the player's
  trust, not their reflexes.
- No map redesign — small additions only.
- Pins never become required.
- Don't edit `storyline.md` / `http-error-codes.md` to match the game.
